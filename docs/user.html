User guide

How to record and analyze a session with your IntoMind device.

Connecting

The Command Center never connects by itself. When it opens with nothing connected, it lists every device it hears, as each one answers, by its name and its address, with the one you picked last time first. When two share a name, the later ones are numbered. Click the one you mean, and only that one is connected. Connect lists them again at any time.

The indicator at the top of the window tells you where things stand:

IndicatorMeaning
No deviceNothing is connected. Press Connect and pick your device.
ConnectingThe device you picked is being connected.
Your device, streamingConnected. The traces are live.
RecordingAn experiment is running and data is being written to disk.

While a device is connected the button reads Disconnect. It asks before it lets go, and it waits while a recording runs. You would use it to hand the device to another program, because only one program can hold the link at a time.

One program at a time. If a second program connects to the same device, both may receive every sample twice. Close one of them. Restarting Bluetooth appears to fix this, but only because it drops the other connection.

Detail level

The Basic, Intermediate and Advanced buttons change how much the page shows you. They never change what the device does, and switching between them will not alter a recording in progress.

Every setting your device reports is stated at every level, because what the instrument was set to is part of what a recording means. What changes is what you may alter: Basic states them and changes nothing, Intermediate lets you set the everyday ones, and Advanced adds the diagnostics that are only meaningful on a bench.

BCI settings

Open the settings drawer from the top right. What it holds depends on what your device says it can do.

Connected device

What the device reported about itself: how many channels, its resolution, its firmware version. Read-only.

Configuration

The instrument's actual configuration, read back from the device rather than remembered from what was last requested. If a setting shows as unknown, the device has not answered for it, and the app will say so rather than guess.

SettingWhat it does
GainHow much the signal is amplified before it is digitized. Higher gain resolves smaller signals but leaves less room before the input saturates.
Sample rateSamples per second, per channel. A higher rate captures faster activity and produces larger recordings.
Signal sourceWhat the amplifier is listening to. Normally the electrodes. A test signal or a shorted input is a bench check of the instrument rather than a recording of you.
Contact detectionWhether the device watches for an electrode coming off. See below.

Only the settings your device reports are shown. A device that offers fewer of them shows fewer, and one that offers something this guide does not list shows that too, under the name the device gave it.

Electrode contact

This appears only if your device can detect an electrode coming off. Press Check electrode contact to test each electrode once. The check briefly passes a very small current through the electrodes, which you will not feel.

You can also leave detection on, from the Configuration list, and then the page tells you the moment an electrode comes off during a recording. The cost is that the same small current flows the whole time, and it disturbs the signal the electrodes are measuring. On a marginal electrode it can flatten a channel entirely. That is why it is off unless you turn it on.

Two independent indicators are reported separately, and they can disagree. That disagreement is information, not a fault: one detects an electrode that is completely disconnected, the other looks at the voltage the skin contact develops. A pair of electrodes can be in contact and still show a small voltage difference, so a low reading does not on its own mean a bad contact.

Model

This appears only if your device carries a model. A model turns a few seconds of signal into an answer, on the device, without sending anything anywhere. The answer comes from a head, which is a small set of weights trained for one question, and your device holds several of them in numbered slots.

Pick a head, turn predictions on, and the output appears under the traces with the head that produced it. If the window it was computed from had a gap in it, or an electrode was off, the page says so beside the number, because that output is worth less than one from a clean window.

Heads are trained from a recording with the instrument library and uploaded from there. There is no way to train one from this page, and there should not be: what a head does on your device has to match what it does on the host exactly, and one implementation is how that stays true.

Running an experiment

Choose a protocol in the Experiment panel on the left, set its parameters, and press Run experiment. Cued protocols speak their instructions aloud, so you can keep your eyes closed or fixed where the protocol asks.

An experiment never changes your instrument settings. It records at whatever the device is configured to, so the settings you chose are the settings you get.

Markers let you timestamp something during a recording. Each name you add becomes a button, and pressing it records the moment you pressed it.

Stop ends a run early. What was recorded up to that point is kept and marked as stopped early.

Recordings and analysis

Every run is written to disk before any analysis touches it, along with the full instrument configuration and any event markers. Recordings are listed in the Analysis panel on the right, newest first, with a badge showing whether their contents still match the checksum written when they were recorded.

Select a recording and it is analyzed automatically. The analysis that matches how it was recorded is chosen for you, and a standard summary and frequency spectrum are always computed alongside it. Results are cached, so returning to a recording is immediate however long it is.

You can pick a different analysis from the dropdown. If you choose one that does not match how the recording was made, the page says so, because the result is unlikely to be meaningful. A general recording has no expected analysis, so any choice is legitimate and nothing is said.

Filters and parameters in analysis

These apply to the analysis only. The recording on disk is never changed, so you can analyze the same data several ways without losing anything.

Exporting

Choose what to export, then a format. Six are offered.

FormatWhat it is
NPZA numpy archive: microvolts, both clocks, the marks and the manifest. The natural choice for analysis in Python.
EDF+The clinical container almost everything reads. Sixteen bits wide.
BDF+The same container at twenty-four bits, which is as wide as the record.
CSVPlain text, one row per sample. Large, and readable anywhere.
TSVThe same rows separated by tabs, for places where a comma is a decimal separator.
MATLABMATLAB v7.3, which is an HDF5 file.

After each export the page reports what that file cost, measured on the file it just wrote rather than claimed about the format: how many samples it holds, whether every sample came back at the value it was recorded at, and the largest error if not. A recording that fails its own checksum is not exported at all.

Data exports the recording. Analysis exports the result you are looking at. Data + analysis exports both.

Deleting a recording

Delete removes the selected recordings permanently, along with their metadata, events and checksums. It cannot be undone, and you will be asked to confirm. Use it for runs that were spoiled, so they cannot later be mistaken for valid data.
